The problem

One wrong answer can hide three different wrong ideas.

A 0.5 kg puck slides across frictionless ice at a constant 8 m/s. What is the net force on it?

Three students all answer

4.0 N forward

correct answer: 0 N

M01
"Something has to keep pushing it, or it would stop."

A force is needed to keep moving

M09
"The hit put a force into the puck that it still carries."

Impetus: the force is stored inside

M12
"It moves forward, so the net force points forward."

Net force always follows the motion

Marking it wrong fixes none of them, and showing the right answer doesn't either. Each student needs a different explanation, so the tutor has to find the idea first.

How it works

From mistake to verified understanding, in five steps.

1

Diagnose

A classifier reads the question, answer and reasoning and names one of 12 misconceptions, with a calibrated confidence.

2

Quick check

When two look-alike ideas are close, it asks the one question that best separates them.

3

Teach

A targeted hint in the mode most likely to work: explanation, force diagram or interactive simulation.

4

Reassess

New transfer questions plus a trap built to catch the old idea. One right answer is never enough.

5

Lock in

A delayed retest a few questions later, then the student explains the idea back in their own words.

Learner model underneath: per-concept mastery and a state for every misconception active→intervened→resolved→relapsed

Multimodal teaching

See it, try it, explain it.

Most adaptive tutors change how hard the next question is. Re:Learn changes how the idea is taught, and a Thompson-sampling policy learns which mode works best for each misconception.

Resolution is earned

One right answer is never proof.

A misconception counts as fixed only after at least two of three transfer questions, a trap aimed at the old idea, and a delayed retest. Passing the first follow-up and failing the trap stays unresolved, and a test makes sure of it.

Training data is synthetic, generated from 48 hand-written templates, and test questions come from templates the model never saw. The definition-matching score is not live yet because it scored lower on validation. Nothing has been validated on real students; a classroom pilot is the next step.
